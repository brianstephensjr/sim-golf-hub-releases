Sim Golf Hub

Privacy Policy

Last updated November 13, 2026

The short version. Sim Golf Hub runs entirely on your own PC and your own Wi‑Fi network. There is no account to create, no cloud service behind it, and no analytics or tracking of any kind. Nothing you do in the app is sent anywhere.

The only connection it ever makes to the internet is a once‑a‑day check for a newer version. Everything else — your layouts, your settings, your logs — stays in a folder on your PC that you can open, inspect, and delete at any time.

Who this is from

Sim Golf Hub is published by Brian Stephens Jr. If you have a question about this policy or about what the app does with your information, contact brian.stephens.jr+simgolfhub@gmail.com.

What Sim Golf Hub does not do

  • It does not require or offer an account, login, or registration.
  • It does not collect analytics, telemetry, usage statistics, or crash reports.
  • It does not upload your settings, layouts, macros, or activity anywhere.
  • It does not record your screen. Mirrors (below) go live to your own devices and are never saved.
  • It does not contain advertising, and it does not sell or share information with anyone.
  • It does not use cookies or tracking technologies to profile you.

Information the app handles, and where it stays

Everything below is stored only on your PC, in your own Windows user profile, in a folder that is usually %LOCALAPPDATA%\SimGolfHub. The Microsoft Store version keeps it with its own app data under %LOCALAPPDATA%\Packages instead, unless the folder was already there from the standalone version. Either way, Config → Data shows where it is, and Open folder opens it.

Devices you connect

When a phone or tablet connects, the app knows its local network address and the name and type the device reports, and shows them on the dashboard so you can see what is connected. The address is held in memory while the device is connected, and connection events are recorded in the local logs described above. The name, the type, the device's screen size and the layout it shows are kept in devices.json, so the app remembers the device next time. None of it is ever transmitted off your PC.

Mirrors of your sim's windows

A mirror shows part of your sim's window, such as its minimap, live on a phone or tablet. Mirroring is off until you turn it on for a profile. While it's on, the app captures a window only while a device or the dashboard is showing one of its mirrors, and only the windows of that profile's own program, never your browser or anything else. The pictures go straight to the devices showing them, over your local network, and are held in memory only as long as it takes to send them. They are never saved and never leave your network. Touches on a mirror come back to the PC as clicks and drags in the sim's window. What's saved is each mirror's setup, in apps\: its name, the title of the window it shows, and the part you selected.

Windows normally draws a yellow border around a window that is being captured. On Windows 11 the app turns that border off; the Microsoft Store version asks you first, and you can change the answer later in Windows Settings.

Optional diagnostic logging

The app has a diagnostic setting, off by default, that records the individual button presses arriving from a controller. It exists so a problem can be traced, it can only be turned on from the PC itself, and what it records is written to the same local log files. Turning it off stops the recording; deleting the logs folder removes what was recorded.

Network connections the app makes

On your local network

Sim Golf Hub runs a small web server on your PC so your phone or tablet can load the controller, send button presses to it, and, with Mirroring on, show its mirrors. It also announces itself on your local network (via mDNS) so devices can find the PC by name instead of by a numeric address. Both are confined to your own network.

Worth knowing: that local connection is ordinary unencrypted HTTP, which is a deliberate choice for a device on your own trusted home network — it avoids the certificate warnings that would otherwise appear on every phone. It is protected by a pairing PIN, unique to your installation, which a device must supply before it can send anything or show a mirror. Mirror pictures travel over this same connection. If you do not trust the network you are on, do not use the app on it.

To the internet: the version check, and nothing else

Once a day the app checks whether a newer version exists. That is the only internet connection it makes, and it works differently depending on which build you installed:

  • Microsoft Store version — it asks Windows whether an update is waiting. Windows handles the request; the app itself contacts no one.
  • Standalone version — it requests the public list of releases from GitHub. The request contains no information about you or your use of the app; like any web request it necessarily reveals your IP address to the server being contacted, which in this case is GitHub's.

Nothing is downloaded or installed automatically, and no personal information is sent either way. If the check is blocked, or you are offline, the app carries on working normally.

Other parties

Sim Golf Hub does not disclose your information to anyone. Three parties are involved only in the limited ways described here:

  • Microsoft — if you installed from the Microsoft Store, Windows performs the update check and manages installation, under Microsoft's own privacy terms.
  • GitHub — if you installed the standalone version, the daily version check reaches GitHub's public API, as described above.
  • Stripe — the app shows an optional donation link. Donations are entirely voluntary and unlock nothing. If you choose to follow it, the link opens in your own web browser and you leave the app; any payment details you enter go to Stripe under Stripe's privacy policy, and Sim Golf Hub never sees or stores them.

How your information is kept

The files listed above live in your Windows user profile and are protected by your Windows account, the same as your other documents. Sim Golf Hub does not encrypt them separately, and does not need to: they never leave the PC, and anyone who can already read your user profile can read your other files too. There is no server, and no copy of your data anywhere else.

The app keeps your data for as long as you keep it. Nothing expires and nothing is uploaded.

Your choices and controls

  • See everything the app has stored — Config → Data → Open folder. It is all plain files, readable and deletable.
  • Erase it — Config → Data → Reset all data deletes your settings, profiles and layouts and starts fresh. To remove everything, delete the folder yourself while the app is closed.
  • Control who can connect — the pairing PIN under Config → Network can be changed or required, so only a device you have given it to can send input.
  • Stop mirroring — Mirroring is off unless you turn it on, on a profile's Mirrors tab. Turning it off there stops all capture for that profile at once.
  • Stop the diagnostic logging — it is off unless you turn it on, and can be turned off again at any time.
  • Stop the version check — there is no in‑app switch for it, but blocking the app in your firewall or going offline prevents it, and the app keeps working. You simply will not be told when a new version is out.
  • Remove the app — uninstall it like any Windows app. If the Microsoft Store version made its own data folder, Windows removes that folder with the app. Otherwise your settings and layouts are deliberately left behind in %LOCALAPPDATA%\SimGolfHub so a reinstall keeps them; delete that folder yourself if you want them gone.
